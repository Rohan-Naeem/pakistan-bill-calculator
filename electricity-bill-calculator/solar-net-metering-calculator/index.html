How to use this Solar Net Metering calculator — step by step

This guidance is specific to Solar Net Metering (Pakistan) (Pakistan (where net metering is approved)). Follow it in order, and the estimate will match the way Solar Net Metering itself builds a bill.

  1. Enter imported units first — the electricity you took from the grid, from your bi-directional meter or bill.
  2. Enter exported units separately — the surplus solar electricity you sent back. Never enter only the difference.
  3. Choose domestic or commercial as per your net-metering connection, then press Calculate.
  4. Read import cost and export credit as separate lines. Export credit is applied only at the official export rate once it is filled in from your approved net-metering terms.

How a Solar Net Metering bill is calculated

A net-metering bill has two consumption figures, not one. Imported units are the electricity you took from the grid, usually at night or on cloudy days. Exported units are the surplus solar electricity you sent back to the grid. The bill credits exported units under the approved terms, then adds fixed charges and taxes on the relevant parts.

Because import and export rates are not necessarily the same, a simple subtraction of export from import does not give the bill. The calculator therefore keeps import and export separate, applies the slab structure to imports, and applies the export credit only at the official export rate once that rate is filled in from the approved net-metering terms.

Credits also have rules about how long they last. A surplus in one month may be carried forward, but it may not last indefinitely. Check the banking period in your agreement before planning to use a large summer surplus in winter.

How to check your Solar Net Metering bill online

  1. Find the reference number on any old bill for your distribution company.
  2. Open that company's official bill-check portal.
  3. Enter the reference number with no spaces or dashes.
  4. Check the import, export, net and total figures separately on the bill that opens.

How to download a duplicate bill

Once the bill opens on the official portal, use your browser's print option and choose Save as PDF, or print it directly. On a phone, use Share, then Print. Keep the PDF for payment and for your records, and check that the reference number, billing month and due date are all visible on the copy.

Complaints and corrections

If a bill looks wrong, work through it in this order: units and readings, category, billing period, arrears, then taxes and adjustments. Most disputes are settled at one of those steps without a formal complaint, because the cause is usually a reading, a category or an arrears figure, not a hidden charge.

If the bill is still wrong after that check, raise a complaint through the official Solar Net Metering complaint channel or your nearest Solar Net Metering office, with your reference or consumer number, the bill month and a dated photo of your meter if the reading is disputed. Helpline numbers are not printed on this page on purpose: they change, and a wrong number here would be worse than no number. Use the contact page on the official Solar Net Metering website linked above for the current number.

Keep a copy of any complaint reference you are given, and keep the bill and the meter photo together until the correction appears on a later bill. If a correction is promised, check the next bill — do not assume it has been applied.

Common mistakes that make an estimate wrong

  • Entering the meter reading instead of the units used that month. Units are present reading minus previous reading.
  • Entering a two-month figure as if it were one month, especially for gas.
  • Choosing the wrong category, or guessing protected status instead of checking the bill.
  • Forgetting arrears or a late surcharge that are already on the official bill.
  • Comparing rupees with last month instead of comparing units, which hides whether usage or rates changed.

A good habit is to write down your units every month, next to the bill total. After three months you will see your own pattern, and a sudden jump will stand out immediately, whether it is usage, a slab change or an error.

Frequently asked questions about Solar Net Metering

How does solar net metering billing work in Pakistan?

With an approved net-metering connection, a bi-directional meter records units imported from the grid and units exported from your solar system. Exported units are credited under the approved terms, and the bill is based on the net position plus fixed charges and taxes. The exact credit rate and banking rules come from your net-metering agreement.

What numbers do I need for this solar calculator?

You need imported units and exported units for the billing period, from the bi-directional meter or the bill. Do not enter only the difference, because import and export are treated separately in net metering.

What happens if I export more than I import?

In a month where exports exceed imports, the surplus is normally carried forward as a credit under the approved rules, rather than paid out as cash. The banking period and credit expiry depend on the agreement, so check those terms before assuming a credit lasts forever.

Does this calculator give my exact net-metering bill?

No, it gives an estimate once the official import, export and fixed-charge rates are filled in. Your actual bill also depends on arrears, taxes and the specific terms of your approved connection.

Do I need approval before installing net metering?

Yes. Net metering is not automatic when solar panels are installed. It needs an approved connection and a bi-directional meter through your distribution company, under NEPRA net-metering rules.

Why is my solar bill not zero even when I export a lot?

Fixed charges, taxes and any imported units still appear on the bill, and export credit is applied under the approved rate, which is not necessarily equal to the import rate. A bill can therefore be small without being zero.

How do I check a net-metering bill online?

Use the same official bill-check portal as a normal electricity consumer for your distribution company, with the reference number from your bill. The bill itself should show import, export and net figures separately.

What should I do if export units look missing?

Compare the bi-directional meter readings with the bill, keep a dated photo of the meter, and raise the issue through your distribution company's official complaint channel with the bill month ready.

Can this calculator help me size a solar system?

Only roughly. It estimates a bill from import and export figures you already have. Sizing a system properly needs your hourly and seasonal consumption pattern, roof and panel data, and a site survey, which this calculator does not do.
