    How to calculate electricity bill in Pakistan

    To calculate an electricity bill, start with units in kWh, split them across slabs, add the fixed charge, then add Fuel Price Adjustment, quarterly adjustment, electricity duty, GST and the TV fee. That is the electricity bill calculation formula in Pakistan in words — slabs first, then charges, then taxes — and it applies whether the company is LESCO, FESCO, GEPCO, IESCO, MEPCO, PESCO, HESCO, SEPCO, QESCO, TESCO or K-Electric. WAPDA bill calculation questions usually mean the same thing, because the DISCOs were historically part of WAPDA's distribution system; today you should use your own DISCO's calculator, not a generic WAPDA one.

    Two industrial terms often cause confusion. MDI, or maximum demand indicator, relates to the highest load recorded for some industrial and large commercial connections, and power factor relates to how efficiently that load uses electricity. Neither is a normal domestic bill line, so if they appear on a domestic bill, check the category first. You can also calculate an electricity bill in Excel using the slab-table method explained on the LESCO page, which is the same method this site's calculator uses.

    Why do calculators give different totals for the same 300 units?

    Because a total is not one number — it is a list of lines, and different calculators include different lines. Checked 7 Nov 2026, for the same 300 units, non-protected: this site's calculator gives Rs 12,326.43 at 1 kW, before FPA and quarterly adjustment. Oraan gives Rs 13,267 at 1 kW, including a fuel adjustment of Rs 2.0581 per unit (July 2026) and a quarterly adjustment of Rs 0.5194. Qaaf gives Rs 14,448 at 2 kW, including a fuel adjustment of Rs 1.1086 per unit and a financing-cost surcharge of Rs 3.23 per unit — a different month's fuel adjustment from Oraan's, which is why the two disagree. The official IESCO calculator gives Rs 11,846.40 and says itself that it excludes FPA, electricity duty, NJ surcharge and quarterly adjustment. One rival, lescoebilcheck.pk, gives Rs 11,939.57 using Rs 27.14 per unit, while its own tariff table says Rs 33.10 for 201-300 units. Another, lescobillcheck.pk, admits its rates were valid only until 30 June 2026. Before trusting any total, check four things: which lines are included, what month the FPA and quarterly adjustment are from, what sanctioned load was used, and whether the rates are the current S.R.O. 279(I)/2026 schedule. A total without those answers cannot be compared with another total.

    Sub-meter electricity bill

    An electric sub-meter bill calculator question is usually about a shared connection: one main meter, and a sub-meter for one flat, shop or portion of a house. Take the sub-meter's present reading minus its previous reading — that difference is the sub-meter's units — then work out the bill from those units. The hard part is the slab: if the main meter's total is split across slabs, the sub-meter's share of the higher slabs has to be agreed in writing with the main meter holder before a dispute arises, because the sub-meter alone does not tell you which slab its units fell in. The full method, with a worked split, is in the guide Sub-Meter Electricity Bill: How to Calculate It.

    Electricity unit price in Pakistan and how to calculate a bill by units

    The electricity unit price in Pakistan is not one number. It is a slab table, and the price you actually pay per unit depends on how many units you used that month and whether you are protected or non-protected. For domestic consumers under the verified Feb 2026 schedule, protected is Rs 10.54/kWh up to 100 units and Rs 13.01 for 101-200, while non-protected runs from Rs 22.44 (1-100 units) through Rs 33.10 (201-300) up to Rs 47.20 above 700 units, plus a fixed charge per kW of sanctioned load. Commercial connections up to 5 kW are Rs 37.44/kWh flat, and industrial B1 connections up to 25 kW are Rs 26.23/kWh flat, each with its own fixed charge.

    To calculate an electricity bill by units, do it in this order: split the units across slabs (protected) or take the final band rate for all units (non-protected), add the fixed charge, then add duty, GST and TV fee. Worked example, already checked on this site's calculator: 300 units, non-protected, 1 kW sanctioned load — energy 300 x Rs 33.10 = Rs 9,930, plus fixed Rs 350 and TV fee Rs 35, plus duty Rs 154.73 and GST Rs 1,856.70, for Rs 12,326.43 before that month's FPA and quarterly adjustment. (Corrected 7 Nov 2026: the earlier figure of Rs 12,354.28 charged GST on a duty-inclusive base; this calculator charges GST on energy + fixed charge + TV fee only, and the live calculator itself shows PKR 12,326.425 for these inputs.) That example is why comparing units matters more than comparing rupees: the same 300 units at a different band, or under protected status, gives a very different total.
