    How it works

    1. Units in: you enter the units from your bill or meter.
    2. Slabs applied: units are split across tariff slabs, in order.
    3. Charges added: fixed charges, adjustments and taxes are added line by line.
    4. Result out: you get an estimated bill, a breakdown table and a simple chart, with print, copy and share options.

    Why estimates differ from official bills

    An estimate uses units and category only. An official bill also uses the actual meter reading for that month, arrears, late surcharge, corrections and any adjustment the company applies. If your estimate and your bill differ, compare units first, then category, then arrears — in that order. That sequence solves most differences without a complaint.

    How to calculate electricity bill, gas bill and water bill in Pakistan

    Whether you use a bill calculator, an electricity bill calculator or a gas bill calculator, the method is the same at its core. First, find how much you used. For electricity, that means calculating your bill from the meter reading: present reading minus previous reading gives your units in kWh. You can also calculate electricity bill units from watts: watts multiplied by hours used, divided by 1,000, gives kWh. For gas, the same meter-reading idea applies, but the unit is HM3, and some bills also show MMBTU — the conversion between them is printed on the bill, so use that factor rather than guessing it.

    Second, split those units across slabs. This is the part most people skip, and it is why a simple "units times one rate" calculation is usually wrong. A domestic electricity bill calculator, a commercial electricity bill calculator and an industrial electricity bill calculator all use slabs, but with different rates and fixed charges, so always check the category on your bill first.

    Third, add the other lines one by one: fixed charges, Fuel Price Adjustment, quarterly adjustment where it applies, electricity duty, GST and, for electricity, the TV fee. If you want to understand a single line, the questions to ask are the same ones people search for every day: how to calculate FPA in an electricity bill, how to calculate GST on an electricity bill, how to calculate electricity duty, and how to calculate income tax on an electricity bill. Each one is a separate line with its own base, and the Methodology page explains that order in full.

    A few special cases come up often. A sub-meter electricity bill is calculated from the sub-meter's own reading difference, using the same slab idea, and it should be agreed in writing with the main meter holder before a dispute arises. An AC bill — including a DC inverter AC bill — is not a separate tariff: an air conditioner simply adds kWh, and an inverter AC usually adds fewer kWh than an old non-inverter AC for the same cooling, which is why the appliance matters more than any "AC calculator" label. And a split bill calculator question is really a sharing question: calculate the full bill first, then split it by agreed shares, rather than calculating two separate bills from one meter.

    Finally, remember the difference between an online bill calculator and an online bill check. A calculator estimates a bill from units you enter. A bill check shows the actual bill a company has issued for a reference or consumer number. This site is a calculator. For the actual bill, always use the official portal linked on each company page.
