Why Is My Electricity Bill So High in Summer?

Summer bills rise mainly because cooling adds a lot of kWh, and those extra kWh fall into higher slabs. A bill that doubles in summer is often correct in method, even if it feels wrong in amount — but it should still be checked, not assumed correct.

Check in this order

First, compare units with the same month last year, not with last month. Second, check category and protected status. Third, check the meter readings against your own photo. Fourth, check FPA and taxes. Only then consider a complaint.

What actually saves units

Higher thermostat settings, fans with AC, clean filters, and shorter heater/geyser hours in winter all save more than switching off lights. An old non-inverter AC is often the single largest summer load in a home.
