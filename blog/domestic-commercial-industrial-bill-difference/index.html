Domestic vs Commercial vs Industrial Electricity Bill in Pakistan

Category is the single biggest hidden variable in an electricity bill. A domestic, commercial and industrial connection with the same units will not produce the same bill, because each category has its own slab structure and fixed charges. For the same 300 units: domestic non-protected is Rs 12,326.43 before FPA and quarterly adjustment, a commercial connection up to 5 kW comes to Rs 14,688.94, and an industrial B1 connection up to 25 kW comes to Rs 10,598.46 — same units, three different totals.

Domestic

Domestic bills use the protected / non-protected slab system, with fixed charges for homes. They are designed for household use, not for running a shop or a workshop from the same connection.

Commercial and industrial

Commercial and industrial bills use different rates and, for larger connections, demand-related concepts such as sanctioned load and MDI. Running commercial load on a domestic connection is a common cause of disputes, back-billing and category correction.

Same 300 units, three different bills

The table below works out 300 units for each category, line by line, exactly as the calculators on this site do. Rates are the uniform national schedule from NEPRA S.R.O. 279(I)/2026 (Feb 2026): domestic non-protected 300 units fall in the 201–300 band at Rs 33.10/kWh with a fixed charge of Rs 350/kW, commercial up to 5 kW is a flat Rs 37.44/kWh with a Rs 1,000 fixed charge, and industrial B1 up to 25 kW is a flat Rs 26.23/kWh with a Rs 1,000 fixed charge. Domestic assumes 1 kW of sanctioned load. Electricity duty is 1.5% and GST is 18%, both on energy + fixed charge + TV fee; the TV fee is Rs 35 for domestic, Rs 60 for commercial, and does not apply to industrial.

Totals exclude that month's Fuel Price Adjustment and quarterly adjustment, which change monthly and must be taken from your bill, and exclude arrears and surcharges. The gap between categories is mostly the energy rate: at 300 units the commercial rate (Rs 37.44) is higher than the domestic band rate (Rs 33.10), while the industrial rate (Rs 26.23) is lower than both — so the industrial total comes out lowest even though its fixed charge is the same Rs 1,000 as commercial. A protected domestic consumer would pay less still, because protected slabs are Rs 10.54/kWh for 1–100 units and Rs 13.01 for 101–200, but protected status ends above 200 units, so it does not apply at 300 units.

Check the category on your own bill

The category is printed on the bill, normally near the consumer details — domestic, commercial or industrial, and protected or non-protected for domestic. If the category on the bill does not match how the connection is actually used, the estimate from any calculator will not match the bill, and that is the first thing to check before disputing the units or the reading. Use the electricity bill calculator for your company, choose the category exactly as printed on the bill, and compare the slab-by-slab breakdown with the bill's own lines.
