Watts to Units: How to Convert Watts to kWh

If you know an appliance's watts and how long it runs, you can work out its kWh without waiting for a bill. The formula is watts multiplied by hours, divided by 1,000. That result is kWh, which is the same as 'units' on an electricity bill.

Example in words

A 1,000-watt appliance running for one hour uses one kWh. A 100-watt appliance running for ten hours also uses one kWh. Add up each appliance's kWh for the month, and you have an estimate of your total units before the bill arrives.

Using the estimate

Compare your watts-based estimate with the units on your last bill. If they are close, your appliance list is probably complete. If they differ a lot, an appliance you forgot — a heater, a geyser, an old fridge — is usually the missing piece.
